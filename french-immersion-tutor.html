Signs

When immersion gets hard.

Many children sail through the early years of immersion, then struggle when reading, writing and subject content in French ramp up. Common signs:

  • "I hate French," or avoiding homework
  • Falling behind in more than one subject because of the language
  • Anxiety about schoolwork
  • A parent who wants to help but doesn't speak French

Read more in our parent's guide to French immersion.

Language or learning?

Language gap or learning gap?

A language gap means your child understands the idea but can't access it in French: they get the math, but not the French word problem. That's where a tutor helps most. A learning gap shows up in any language, and that's a conversation with your child's school.

FAQ

French immersion questions, answered.

Do I need to speak French to help my child?

No. Many immersion parents don't speak French. Our free parent kit gives you a 10-minute routine you can follow in English, and the tutor works with your child in French.

At what grade do kids struggle most in immersion?

Commonly around Grades 3–4, when reading and writing ramp up, and again around Grade 7 with more abstract content. Both are normal inflection points.

Is it a language gap or a learning gap?

A language gap means your child understands the idea in English but gets stuck on the French. A learning gap shows up in any language, and that's a conversation with the school. A tutor helps most with a language gap.
