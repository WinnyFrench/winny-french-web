What it is

Core French is a subject. Immersion is a school day.

Core French is the program, common in English-language schools, where French is one class on the timetable. Math, science, and the rest of the day stay in English. That is the honest difference from French immersion, where other subjects are taught in French, and from extended French, where a board teaches French plus at least one other subject in French.

How much French a student gets, and which grade it starts, depends on the province and the board.

  • In many Ontario boards, core French starts in Grade 4 and is compulsory through Grade 9. After that it is optional, through Grade 12.
  • Some boards start earlier. Ottawa's public board teaches core French through the elementary English program, not only from Grade 4.
  • In Calgary, students who are not in immersion may take French as a second language as a course. Pathways can start in Grade 4, Grade 7, or Grade 10, depending on the school.
  • New Brunswick's anglophone schools do not use the Ontario core French model. They use French immersion, and an intensive French block (commonly in Grade 5) followed by post-intensive French.

Winny French is based in Ontario. Classes are online across Canada, for core French students in Grades 1 to 12, including the early grades in boards that start before Grade 4 and the high school years when a student is deciding whether to keep French.

Why it gets hard

One French period is not much French.

A core French class is often the only French a student hears all day. There is less time to speak than in immersion, and homework can pile up on grammar or a piece of writing the class did not finish. A low mark is often a practice problem, not a sign that French "isn't for them."

You do not need to speak French to help. The tutor works with your child on the work the school is grading. You do not have to teach the lesson yourself.

What a class works on

  • The homework, grammar, and vocabulary the class is on now
  • Listening and speaking, which a short period may not leave much time for
  • Reading and writing at the student's grade
  • High school French, including the optional courses after Grade 9 in Ontario
  • A test or an oral presentation the student is worried about

FAQ

Core French questions, answered.

What is core French?

Core French is the program, common in English-language schools, where French is one subject. The rest of the day is in English. French immersion is different: other subjects are taught in French. Extended French, where a board offers it, teaches French plus at least one other subject in French. How many minutes, and which grade it starts, depends on the province and the board.

Which grades do you tutor?

Grades 1 to 12. In many Ontario boards core French starts in Grade 4, and some boards start earlier. We also help high school students in the years when French is optional.

Do I need to speak French to help my child?

No. Many core French parents don't speak French. The tutor works with your child on the school French. You don't have to teach the lesson yourself.

Is this the same as immersion tutoring?

No. Immersion students are taught other subjects in French, so the gap often shows up in those subjects as well as in French class. Core French students are graded on the French subject itself. If your child is in immersion, the French immersion tutoring page is the better fit.

How much does core French tutoring cost?

Small groups of 2 to 3 students are $25 per student per hour. One-on-one is $35 per hour, or $30 per hour at 2 or more sessions a week. There is a free 30-minute trial class. There is no registration fee. A free class if there's no improvement in 4 weeks.

When are classes, and do you serve families outside Ontario?

Classes run Monday to Friday from 4pm to 9pm, and anytime on weekends, Eastern time. Winny French is based in Ontario, and classes are online across Canada. If you are in Western Canada, our tutors do their best to find a time that works in your time zone. Weekday hours stay on the Eastern clock.

Do you follow my child's curriculum?

For Ontario students, tutors work from the Ontario French as a Second Language curriculum and from the homework the teacher assigned. For students in other provinces, the tutor works from that student's own classwork. We don't claim to hold every board's internal lesson plans.
