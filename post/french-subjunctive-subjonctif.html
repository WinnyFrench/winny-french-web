The French Subjunctive (Subjonctif): When to Use It and How to Form It

Nov 13, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer: The subjunctive (le subjonctif) is not a tense, it is a mood: a special verb form French uses after certain expressions of wish, emotion, doubt, necessity and judgment. It almost always shows up in a second clause introduced by que. English barely has it anymore, which is exactly why it feels strange. You form it by taking the ils form of the present, dropping the -ent, and adding a fixed set of endings. A short list of common verbs is irregular.

First: English used to have this too

The subjunctive feels alien because English has mostly lost it, but a few fossils survive. The clearest one is the expression "if I were you." Think about it: for "I" in the past, the normal verb is "I was." Yet nobody says "if I was you" in careful English, they say "if I were you." That odd "were" is the English subjunctive hanging on in a set phrase. It signals something unreal or hypothetical, which is exactly the job the subjunctive does in French.

There are a few more once you start noticing them. "I wish I were taller." "The teacher insists that he be on time" (not "is"). "It is essential that she arrive early" (not "arrives"). English speakers use these without thinking about it. French simply uses the same kind of form far more often, and marks it with its own set of endings.

What the subjunctive actually is

A mood is the attitude a verb takes toward reality. Most of the time you use the indicative, the "this is a fact" mood: il est là (he is here). The subjunctive is the "this is in my head, not a stated fact" mood. It covers things you want, fear, doubt, or judge rather than things you are reporting as true. Wish, necessity, emotion, doubt, and judgment are its home territory.

When to use it: two conditions plus a trigger

The subjunctive is not something you choose for a feeling. It is triggered by specific words, and two structural conditions almost always hold.

1. There is a que. The subjunctive lives in a dependent clause introduced by que (or qui). If the sentence instead uses de plus an infinitive, you do not use the subjunctive.

FrenchEnglish
Il est important que tu fasses tes devoirs.It is important that you do your homework.
Il est important de faire tes devoirs.It is important to do your homework.

2. The two subjects are different. The subject of the main clause and the subject of the que clause must be different people. If they are the same person, French drops the subjunctive and uses an infinitive instead.

FrenchEnglish
Je veux partir.I want to leave. (same subject, infinitive)
Je veux que tu partes.I want you to leave. (two subjects, subjunctive)

How to form the subjunctive: the regular rule

The good news: after all that, the conjugation is mostly easy. For regular -er, -ir and -re verbs, and many irregular ones too, follow three steps:

  1. Take the ils / elles form of the present tense.
  2. Drop the -ent to get the stem.
  3. Add the subjunctive endings: -e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent.
Subjectparler (parl-)finir (finiss-)vendre (vend-)

Two things worth noticing. The nous and vous forms look exactly like the imparfait (parlions, parliez). And for -er verbs, the je, tu, il and ils forms look identical to the ordinary present, so if you are unsure, an -er verb often hides the difference for you.

Notice that aller and vouloir are "boot" verbs: the nous and vous forms (allions, alliez, voulions, vouliez) drop back to a regular-looking stem, while the other four forms use the special one (aille, veuille). One more to know is the impersonal falloir, which exists only in the il form: il faut becomes qu'il faille.

Putting it together

FrenchEnglish
Il faut que tu sois à l'heure.You have to be on time.

Bien qu'il soit fatigué, il travaille.Although he is tired, he works.
Il est important que vous ayez un passeport.It is important that you have a passport.
Je doute qu'elle puisse venir.I doubt that she can come.

One more thing: there is no future subjunctive

You never need a future form. Even when the meaning is clearly about the future, French uses the present subjunctive: Il faut que tu prennes le bus demain means "You will have to take the bus tomorrow." Present subjunctive, future meaning.

Frequently asked questions

What is the French subjunctive?

The subjunctive is a mood, not a tense. It is a verb form French uses after expressions of wish, emotion, doubt, necessity and judgment, almost always in a clause introduced by que. It marks something subjective or uncertain rather than a stated fact.

Does English have a subjunctive?

English has a few leftovers. The clearest is "if I were you," where the subjunctive "were" replaces the normal "was" to signal something unreal. Others include "I wish I were taller" and "it is essential that she arrive on time." English uses these without marking them clearly, while French uses the subjunctive far more often and with its own endings.

How do you form the French subjunctive?

Take the ils form of the present tense, drop the -ent to get the stem, and add the endings -e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent. For example, ils finissent gives the stem finiss-, so que je finisse, que tu finisses, and so on. A small set of common verbs, including être, avoir, aller, faire, pouvoir, savoir and vouloir, are irregular.

What expressions are followed by the subjunctive?

Triggers of wish, necessity and advice (il faut que, vouloir que, il est important que), of emotion and judgment (être content que, avoir peur que, il est dommage que), and of doubt and possibility (douter que, il est possible que, ne pas penser que). Certain conjunctions always take it too: bien que, pour que, avant que, jusqu'à ce que, à moins que and sans que.

When do I use the infinitive instead of the subjunctive?

When the two clauses have the same subject. "Je veux partir" (I want to leave) keeps the infinitive because one person does both actions. "Je veux que tu partes" (I want you to leave) needs the subjunctive because the subjects are different.

Is there a future subjunctive in French?

No. French has no future subjunctive. Even for clearly future situations, you use the present subjunctive: "Il faut que tu prennes le bus demain" means "You will have to take the bus tomorrow."
