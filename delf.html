The diploma

What the DELF is.

The DELF (Diplôme d'études en langue française) is an official French diploma for people whose first language is not French. It is issued under the French Ministry of National Education and designed by France Éducation international. There are four separate diplomas, A1, A2, B1, and B2, lined up with the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages. Each one assesses four skills: listening, reading, writing, and speaking. The diploma does not expire.

You register for one level at a time. A strong result at one level does not replace the next one. We do not publish task timings, fees, or a passing score here, because the exam centre sets those details and they can change. Confirm them with the centre before you book a sitting.

Teenagers

What DELF Junior is.

DELF Junior, also called DELF Junior / Scolaire, is the version for secondary-school-age teenagers, commonly described as about ages 12 to 18. It uses the same levels, A1 to B2, and the same four skills. The topics are written for teenagers rather than for adult life at work. Exam centres describe this version as having the same value as the general-public DELF. The diploma itself is still a DELF.

Two neighbouring exams are easy to mix up, and this page is not about them. DELF Prim is a version for younger children. The DALF is the higher pair of diplomas, at C1 and C2. If that is what your family is considering, say so on the trial form and we will tell you honestly whether a tutor can help.

Some Canadian boards give older students a chance to take the DELF, sometimes in the last year of high school. Whether your board does, and in which grade, is the board's decision. Ask the school. We don't keep a list of which boards offer a sitting this year.

How tutoring helps

Practice, with feedback. Not a promised result.

A tutoring class is practice before the exam. It is not the exam, and it is not a predicted score. In a typical hour the tutor works on the level you are aiming for:

  • Listening and reading, so the student gets used to following French without translating every word
  • Writing, with corrections that show the pattern behind a mistake
  • Speaking, out loud, which a school period may not leave much time for
  • One priority to practice before the next class

School French and the DELF are related, but they are not the same task. A student in core French or French immersion can prepare for the diploma alongside the school course. The tutor uses the schoolwork and the level the family is aiming for. We don't replace the school's program.

Some tutors are DELF certified. That means they hold a DELF diploma. It does not mean they are examiners. Winny French is not an exam centre.

TEF Canada and TCF Canada are different exams, often used for immigration. That preparation is on the TEF Canada and TCF Canada page. Winny French does not have a dedicated TEF/TCF specialist.
